How we cut

Six rules we
do not break

The difference between an edit that gets watched and one that gets scrolled is almost never the effects.

The first three seconds

If the opening frame does not create a question, nothing after it matters. Every cut starts by deciding what makes someone stop.

Pacing over polish

Dead air is what loses viewers, not a missing transition. We cut the pauses, the restarts and the throat-clearing before adding anything.

Sound is half the video

Levelled dialogue, removed noise, a music bed that sits under the voice instead of fighting it, and effects that land on the cut.

Colour with intent

A grade that matches the brand and stays consistent across every clip, so the feed looks like one channel rather than a folder of exports.

Captions people can read

Styled to the brand, timed to speech, positioned inside the safe zone on every platform — because most of the audience is watching on mute.

Cut for the platform

A YouTube edit is not a Reel with the sides cropped. Each format gets its own pacing, framing and caption treatment.

Questions

Before you send footage

How fast is the turnaround?
Reels and Shorts are typically 24–72 hours from receiving the footage. Long-form YouTube and podcast episodes usually run 3–6 days depending on length and how much B-roll and motion work is involved. Event and highlight films take longer because of the volume of footage. If something is urgent, say so up front and it can be prioritised.
How do I send the footage?
Google Drive, WeTransfer or Dropbox — whatever you already use. Send the raw files rather than anything pre-compressed, and include any logos, fonts, brand colours or music you want used. If you are shooting on a phone, we can tell you the settings to use beforehand so the footage grades properly.
How many revisions do I get?
We work until it is right rather than counting rounds. In practice most projects settle in one or two passes because the direction gets agreed before the edit starts. What we ask is that feedback comes in one consolidated pass rather than trickling in over several days.
Can you write the script or hooks too?
Yes. Hashir writes scripts and teleprompter-formatted read-throughs, including for on-camera pieces recorded in awkward setups. If you have the idea but not the words, that is a normal part of the job rather than an add-on.
Do you handle the posting and captions as well?
We can. Social media management, posting schedules and campaign work are covered on the Design & Ads page, and a lot of clients have us cut the video and run the account together so the two are actually aligned.
